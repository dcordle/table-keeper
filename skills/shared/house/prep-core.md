# Prep core

How table-keeper builds and checks a session. Read it when preparing,
building or reviewing a session, an encounter or a scenario, and when
running the world after a session. The campaign profile (`profile.md`)
supplies the numbers. A campaign's own templates and documents win over
this file where they say more.

## Who the story is about

- **Story center `world`:** the job and the world lead. Skip the original
  skills' per-PC arc stages and spotlight history. Hooks point at a role
  (an oath, a debt, a skill, a trade), never at a named character or at
  whoever is newest. A personal plot is one optional scene with its own
  context, and the job finishes without it.
- **Story center `characters`:** use the original skills' arc and spotlight
  steps as written.
- File prep under the profile's session unit wherever the original skills
  ask for a chapter or a session.

## Standalone sessions

For `open table` and `drop-in` formats:

- A newcomer understands the job, the place, the stakes and a first move
  within two minutes of the briefing. The job is stated on screen, by a
  card or an NPC, never left to a player to explain.
- No earlier attendance, particular character, or item or fact from
  another session is needed.
- The brief says what counts as done, so the session can end even if the
  threat survives or the job fails.
- Players bring their own characters. Ignore the original one-shot advice
  to pre-generate or pre-tie them.
- When `World taste` is `yes`, open with three or four sentences on the
  world as it stands now, drawn from recent public news or events in the
  record. It is about the world, never about what earlier groups did.
- Give the group a face to turn to: a contact or faction face who helps or
  trades. Recognizing a returning character is flavor and never needed.

## Places and people

- Organize prep by places and people, not by scripted scenes.
- Each place: entrances and links, the read-aloud for arriving or for
  something happening there, who is there, the evidence there tagged with
  the fact it supports, checks and DCs written in the place, and hazards.
- A place may have two states, such as Quiet and Alerted or Day and Night:
  what flips it and what the group sees in each.
- Each person: what they want, know, share freely, what it takes to get
  the rest, and what they hide or lie about (the house NPC rule).

## Clues and obstacles

- Every fact the group must learn to finish has three clues that do not
  share a person, locked room or check. Each fact's clues are reachable
  within two or three moves of the group's first lead.
- Every obstacle the session needs passed has three ways past.
- At least one ending needs no combat.

## Travel

Follow the profile's `Travel` field:

- `cut`: travel is a cut unless it matters.
- `nodes`, `hexes` or `pointcrawl`: write the route from the start point to
  the job through the map's nodes or hexes, each with its danger word or
  hazard. Roll travel complications only as the profile says. If a route
  crosses more than two dangerous stretches, offer a shorter route or one
  roll for the whole trip.

## Rumors

When `Rumors per session` is above 0, write that many rumors. Each is one
line in the voice of someone in the world, tied to a place and a faction,
and points to a possible future session. Give each a person or place where
the group can hear it. A rumor heard in play is logged for later and never
changes the session being played.

## Encounters

- Build the main fight once, inside the profile's `Main fight band`, for
  the profile's party size at the session's build level. Write the total
  XP against each budget the band names. Never rescale to the roster.
- When `Risk note` is `yes`, add one line on how the fight plays at the
  lowest and highest level that may join.
- Flag a fight as swingy when one of these holds, and say which:
  - a save-or-lose or save-or-drop effect at the lowest eligible level;
  - one creature's turn can drop a lowest-level character from full;
  - a solo creature with no legendary or extra actions against the whole
    party, which the action economy turns trivial;
  - every enemy shares one weakness the party is likely to have.
- Apply the profile's `Monster hit points` and `Boss standard`. Label every
  homebrew creature, changed stat block and reskin as such.
- Say who comes if the fight gets loud, and how the group can pull back or
  end it without killing.

## Session load

The original skills never estimate scene durations. Keep that for single
scenes, but check the whole session against the profile's
`Session length`. Count the main fight, other fights, social and
investigation places, travel legs and the debrief. If the plan holds more
than the length fits, say what to cut first: a place, a clue handed over,
a shorter route, or starting the confrontation closer.

## Before delivering

Walk the prep with four groups: one that skips the obvious entrance, one
that misses the first clue, one that treats an opponent as an ally, and
one that withdraws. Each still reaches an end. Confirm every
`Required elements` item from the profile is there.

## After the session

- Run the world only after a completed table session.
- Order: the group's result and any place changes, then faction changes,
  then logged rumors (retire any gone stale, with a visible sign), then
  whether a world event is due.
- "Nothing changed" is a valid result. Say so plainly.
- Prepared places and people that went unused go back to their place's
  notes for a later session.
