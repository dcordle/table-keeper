# Evaluator notes, 2026-11-24

## Q1
Y is far more usable at the table. It gives a full five-scene plan with read-aloud, NPC blocks, a briefing that hands a newcomer the job and a first move, a route with rolls, three rumors tied to places and factions, a load check and four test crews. X leaves every scene as a one-line "apprentice guess" under Open Questions, keeps per-PC arc stages and spotlight hooks, and gives no read-aloud, load check or walkthrough, so the GM would still have to write the session. Neither one fully meets the NPC-block or three-clue standard: Y's Pell has no way to get his secret, and only the main fact gets three clues.

## Q2
Both are runnable, in-band (X 1,900 rising to 2,100, Y 1,900), built for five and clearly labelled. Y is a bit stronger. It checks swinginess on three explicit counts, gives three ways in and three clue sources, lays out a clear Ringing/Quiet state, and runs a walkthrough. X has a tighter single room with good complications and an earned rescue, but it spends space on how the fight plays for four characters, which the table does not need.

## Q3
Both give a complete want/know/share/earn/hide block, gate the rope secret well and keep her from explaining the chapel unprompted. Each also slips in several quoted lines. Y is slightly easier to run: its table tags each fact with how she gives it up, and its run-throughs and physical proof (the rope tin) are useful. X's frontmatter dump takes up space a GM doesn't need at the table.

## Q4
Both correctly leave real time and the play-by-post scene out and move both factions with visible signs. X is more usable because it works in order: crew result and node, then factions, then rumors, then the world event check. It also lists plainly what did not change. Y covers the same moves and updates the Job Board cards where players will see them, but it skips the rumor and world-event steps.

## Q5
Both give a plain "no" without per-scene minutes. Y is more usable: it gives a numbered cut order (route roll, places, a clue handed over in the briefing, one side fight, start the main fight closer) and shows the result fits. X's cuts are sound, but it gives them no order. Neither suggests a single roll or a shorter route; both only drop to two rolls.

## Q6
Both find the fight 25 XP under the band, flag Drowning Song as save-or-drop and fix it with two zombies to 1,200 XP. X is a little more usable: it names the solo action-economy problem outright, and its Song rewrite (light damage plus a pull toward the Widow) removes the swing more cleanly than Y's party-wide Incapacitated. Y's save-odds table is a nice touch, but it never flags the solo-creature problem.
