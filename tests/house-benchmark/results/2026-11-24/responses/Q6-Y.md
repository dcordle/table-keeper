# Bog Widow check (Grey Ferry, level 3 job)

**Short answer: no, not as written.** It misses your budget band on paper, and in play it is two different fights stapled together: the claws and hit points make it easy, and Drowning Song can drop half the table in one turn. Ignore the XP number on this one; the Song is what decides the fight.

## The budget

Your profile builds the main fight above Moderate and at or below High for five characters at the job's level (D&D 2024 budgets, `systems/dnd-5e-2024/session-procedures.md:11`). At level 3 that is:

- Moderate: 5 × 225 = 1,125 XP
- High: 5 × 400 = 2,000 XP
- Bog Widow: 1,100 XP

So on paper it sits 25 XP under the floor. That is a small miss and easy to fix. The real problem is below.

## How it actually plays

**The claws are soft.** Two claws at +6 against a typical level 3 AC of about 16 hit a bit over half the time, so about 7 or 8 damage a round on average. That is below what a CR 4 should deal, and nobody at the table will fear it.

**The body is soft.** AC 13 and 90 HP is closer to a CR 2 or 3 defense. Five level 3 characters will take it down in about two or three rounds.

**Drowning Song is a save-or-drop on everyone.** At level 3 the Con saves against DC 14 look roughly like this:

| Character | Likely Con save | Fails DC 14 |
|---|---|---|
| Fighter (proficient) | +5 | 40% |
| Cleric, wizard | +2 | 55% |
| Rogue | +1 | 60% |

With five characters inside 30 feet, expect two or three to go to 0 on any one use. The chance that nobody drops is about 2%. It can be used on round one (it starts charged) and comes back a third of the time after that, so over a three-round fight it lands about one and a half to two times. Two uses can put the whole crew on the floor with the Widow still clawing at the ones making death saves, and the claws then auto-crit on anyone unconscious within 5 feet (2024 Unconscious rules), which means failed death saves fast.

**At the tier's lowest and highest level:** the job is level 3, so the lowest is the budget level and the numbers above hold. If you open it to levels 4 and 5 like the other board cards, the claws and HP get more trivial, but the Song barely changes, since only the proficient saver gains +1. It stays a coin flip per character at every level the table can bring.

**Risk note: swingy.** The likely outcomes are "easy win" or "near wipe," with little in between, and which one you get depends on two dice rolls at the start.

## Smallest fix

Keep the claws, HP and AC. Change the Song so it hurts and slows without ending anyone's turn for good, and add two drowned dead to clear the budget.

> **Drowning Song (Recharge 5–6).** *Homebrew.* Each creature of the Widow's choice within 30 feet that can hear it makes a DC 13 Constitution saving throw. On a failed save, a target takes 13 (3d8) cold damage, coughs up marsh water and has the Incapacitated condition until the end of its next turn. On a successful save, it takes half as much damage only.

- Three or four hits of 13 across the party is a real scare at level 3 (most have 20 to 30 HP), but it does not drop anyone from full, and the lost turn is the fear beat you wanted without the wipe.
- Add **two Zombies** (CR 1/4, 50 XP each, `monsters-cr0-1.md:202`, used as written) rising from the shallows when the Song first sounds. They fit the Drowned Chapel's own travel line and give the fighter something to hold back while others reach the Widow.
- New total: 1,100 + 100 = **1,200 XP**, inside the band (above 1,125, at or under 2,000).

If you would rather keep a scarier Song, the next-best option is to keep it save-or-fall but make the fail "falls Prone and is Incapacitated, repeat the save at the end of each turn, an ally can spend an action to haul you out." That is harsher than the version above and still never takes someone straight to 0.

## One assumption

I treated this as the Drowned Chapel job (The Bell Under the Water), since a drowning monster fits there, and budgeted at level 3 as you said even though the board cards list levels 3 to 5. Nothing above changes if it is a different job.
