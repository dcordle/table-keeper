# Old Ma Tessaly, for The Bell Under the Water

Old Ma Tessaly already exists in the vault as an AUTHORITATIVE note from Session 2: an eel-seller at the Market Stair, a girl in the chapel's parish before it sank, who wants her grandson, a Reedwives punter, kept out of the chapel, and who knows the bell rope was cut on purpose and by whom. Everything below builds on that note without changing it. The new material is DRAFT until you confirm it.

Three calls I made because the record leaves them open:

- **Who cut the rope:** her mother, Hester Tessaly, a Reedwife of that day who broke with the coven. Swap in anyone you prefer; the rest still works.
- **The grandson:** his name is Cobb, and he is the punt-boy who came back on Tuesday. If you'd rather he stay out of the job, make him the one who is now being asked to punt the Reedwives out there.
- **What is really ringing the bell** is left to the dispatch. Tessaly knows what she heard and what she believes, nothing more.

Market Stair is one link from the Ferryhouse and its danger word is Quiet, so the crew can reach her on the way out to the Reedwater. She is one source among three for anything the crew must learn. The dispatch should put the same facts somewhere else as well (Cobb, the chapel itself, the Reedwives' purchases), so a crew that walks past her stall can still finish.

## At a glance

**Old Ma Tessaly.** A small, bent woman in her late fifties who looks seventy. She sells live eels from tubs at the bottom of the Market Stair and has done so since before most of the town was born. She is wry, cheap with words and dear with prices, and she was a child in the drowned chapel's parish.

**Where:** her stall at the Market Stair (Quiet) by day. At night, when the bell rings, she sits on the top step of the stair with a shuttered lantern, looking out toward the Reedwater.

**Her face for the crew:** she is a neighbor, not an employer. She won't hire them, but she will trade what she knows for one thing.

## Read-aloud

**Arriving at her stall (day):**

> At the bottom of the Market Stair, where the stone goes green and the water slaps the last step, three tubs of black eels churn under a plank counter. The counter is new wood, pale and still smelling of the saw, nailed onto old legs that are scorched to charcoal. Behind it a little woman in a man's oilskin brings a wooden mallet down on an eel's head without looking, and says the price before anyone asks.

**At night, when the bell rings:**

> The stalls are shut and the stair is dark except for one lantern, half-shuttered, on the top step. Out over the Reedwater a sound comes up through the water rather than the air, a low iron note that you feel in your teeth. The old eel-seller sits beside her lantern with her shawl over her head and her eyes on the reeds. She is counting under her breath.

## Playing her

- **Speech:** talks prices the whole time, even when she is telling you something that matters. When she means it, she drops the price: "Two for a penny. One for nothing, if you'll listen."
- **Tell:** brings the mallet down on the board when she wants a subject closed.
- **Instinct:** stalls, haggles and changes the subject. She never fights and never runs. Cornered, she goes quiet and sells eels loudly to the next customer.

## What she wants

- **Right now:** Cobb off the Reedwives' punts and nowhere near the chapel. Since Tuesday he has not slept, and Sister Wren has been by twice asking for him by name.
- **This job:** for someone to make the bell stop, as long as stopping it does not mean ringing it.
- **Longer:** for the chapel to stay sunk and the parish to stay asleep. She buried no one out there; they are all still in the pews.

**What she has:** a hand-span of old tarred bell rope, cut square, that Hester kept and Tessaly keeps in a tin under the eel tubs. It shows the cut was a blade, not rot. She gives it only with the last secret, and it is the hopeful beat of her part: proof that someone stood against the coven once and kept the parish asleep for forty years.

## What she hides or lies about

- Strangers who ask after Cobb hear he's "off at his aunt's, out past the fens." That is a lie. DC 13 Insight catches it.
- She never says her mother was a Reedwife unless she is giving up the last secret. If someone guesses, she slams the mallet and charges them double.
- She tells Sister Wren nothing, and smiles at her while she does it.

## Under pressure

- **Bribed:** coin buys eels, not secrets. A big enough purse makes her suspicious.
- **Threatened:** she shuts up and the whole stair notices. The eel families turn cold on the crew for the session, and every Market Stair check against them is made at Disadvantage.
- **Shown the Tollgate men are behind it:** she doesn't believe it and says so. "They'd charge the drowned a toll. They wouldn't wake them."

## Ties

- **[[Marrow Pell]]:** buys her eels for the Ferryhouse pot and owes her for three weeks of them. She teases him about it and doesn't mind.
- **[[Sister Wren]]:** sells charms two stalls up. Polite on the surface. Tessaly knows exactly what Wren's coven is.
- **[[The Reedwives]]:** employ Cobb; her mother was one of them. She fears them and won't say so.
- **[[Sergeant Dace]]:** his money paid for her new counter after the market fire. She counts it as the one honest toll the Company ever paid.
- **Cobb Tessaly (new, DRAFT):** her grandson, about fifteen, the punt-boy who came back.
- **Places:** [[Map of the Reach|Market Stair]] (her stall), Drowned Chapel (her childhood parish).

## Run-throughs

- **A crew that skips the Market Stair:** they lose the rope and Hester's story, but the bell, the missing boy and the Reedwives' rope all need to be findable elsewhere in the dispatch (Cobb at the punt-mooring, the chandler, the chapel).
- **A crew that misses her first hint:** she has a second chance at night on the top step, and she'll talk to anyone who sits down beside her lantern.
- **A crew that trusts Sister Wren:** if they tell Tessaly they mean to help ring the bell, she gives up the last secret on the spot, out of fear. That's the moment her whole write-up is built for.
- **A crew that withdraws:** she pays them nothing, but she sends a free eel to the Ferryhouse for the debrief with Cobb's thanks if he is safe, or a shut stall if he isn't.
