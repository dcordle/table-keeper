# Will it fit?

Short answer: not as written. You have fourteen beats in one standalone job: a briefing, three travel rolls, six investigation places, two side fights, three fight phases and the debrief. Three of those beats are full D&D combats (two side fights plus a main fight that runs three phases), and the travel tables can add another one. A 3 to 4 hour open-table job with a new player in the seat has room for one real investigation spine, one side fight at most, and the main fight, with the debrief kept safe at the end. I'm not putting minutes on each beat; that's your call at the table. The problem is the count, not any one beat.

## What the vault says about your three legs

Two jobs on the board are three nodes out from the Ferryhouse, and they roll on different lines of the Map of the Reach:

- **The Bell Under the Water** (Drowned Chapel): Market Stair (Quiet), Reedwater (Dangerous), Drowned Chapel (Deadly).
- **The Ledger Clerk Who Will Not Wake** (Tollgate): Market Stair (Quiet), Old Causeway (Uneasy), Tollgate (Watched). Watched has no complication line on the map, so the third roll has nothing to land on.
- **A Chain Across the Reedwater** is only two legs (Quiet, Dangerous).

I've assumed the Bell job, since it's the one with three rollable legs and the bell rumor came up in Session 2. Tell me if it's another one.

Two things on the Bell route add time you haven't budgeted:

- The Deadly line's 1 raises two zombies, which is a third side fight. The Dangerous line's 1 (leeches) and 2 (holed punt) are scenes of their own too.
- The profile says to roll only when travel matters and never homeward. The Quiet roll at Market Stair is a 1-in-6 toll man and nothing otherwise. That's a safe cut.

## What I'd cut

- **Travel:** roll Reedwater and Drowned Chapel only. If the Deadly roll comes up 1, that zombie fight becomes your side fight and you drop a planned one.
- **Six places down to three or four.** Merge places that do the same job. You still need three clues for each thing the crew must learn, but those clues can sit across fewer places, plus the NPCs. A new player has to follow this from the briefing, and six places is a lot to hold.
- **Two side fights down to one.** Keep the one that gives a clue or shows what the threat can do. The other one can be a sign of the threat with no initiative roll: tracks, a body, a sound.
- **Main fight:** keep the three phases, but let a phase change come from something the crew does or fails to stop. If they're winning, end it early rather than playing through every phase.
- **Debrief:** keep it. If the night runs long, cut a place before you cut the Ferryhouse scene.

## Things the profile requires that the plan doesn't show yet

- **A non-combat ending.** The plan as listed ends in a three-phase fight. The crew needs a way to finish the job without winning it, for example by stopping whatever is ringing the bell instead of killing it. What that way is depends on what is ringing the bell, so it's your call.
- **A hopeful beat.** Somewhere in the job the crew should be able to save or win back something. A punt-boy who went out Tuesday and didn't come back is the obvious place for it, if you want him alive.
- **A risk note** on the main fight: one line on how it plays at level 3 and at level 5.

## Main fight budget (D&D 2024, five characters)

Count all three phases together as one encounter. Profile band is above Moderate and at or below High:

| Build level | Above (Moderate) | At or below (High) |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | 1,125 XP | 2,000 XP |
| 4 | 1,875 XP | 2,500 XP |
| 5 | 3,750 XP | 5,500 XP |

The board lists levels 3 to 5 and the profile doesn't name a build level, so pick the one you build at. If you build at 5, a crew of level 3s will meet a fight nearly three times their High budget. Spread out over three phases, that's swingy. If you expect mixed tables, building at 4 is the safer middle.
