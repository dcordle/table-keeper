# Bog Widow main fight: review

**Short answer: no, not as written.** It misses the main fight band on XP, and Drowning Song makes it swingy at level 3. Both have small fixes.

Assumption: the job is open to levels 3 to 5 like everything else on the board, and it is built at level 3 for five characters, as the profile says.

## The numbers

Budget for five characters at level 3 (D&D 2024 XP table, `session-procedures.md`):

| Budget | Per character | Five characters |
|---|---|---|
| Moderate | 225 | 1,125 |
| High | 400 | 2,000 |

The profile's main fight band is above Moderate and at or below High, so the fight needs **1,126 to 2,000 XP**.

One Bog Widow is **1,100 XP**. That is 25 XP under Moderate, so it sits outside the band, on the easy side.

The XP total hides the real problem, though. The Sly Flourish rule of thumb in the same file calls a fight deadly at levels 1 to 4 when total CR is more than a quarter of total character levels. Five level 3 characters give 15 levels, and a quarter of that is 3.75. A CR 4 solo goes past it. Both readings are correct: the stat block is light, and one ability is heavy.

## Why it is swingy

Two of the swingy flags in the prep core apply:

1. **Save-or-drop at the lowest eligible level.** Drowning Song puts everyone within 30 feet on a DC 14 Constitution save, and anyone who fails drops to 0. Level 3 characters with +0 to +2 Con fail that about 55 to 65 percent of the time. If the Widow wins initiative and the crew is bunched up on a punt, expect two or three characters down before anyone acts. On a 5 or 6 it can do it again. Whether this is a fair fight or a near wipe comes down to two d6 rolls.
2. **Solo with no extra actions.** Without the Song, the Widow is AC 13, 90 HP and one turn per round against five characters. Focused fire takes it down in about three rounds. So the fight either ends on a recharge roll or rolls over quickly, and never lands where you want it.

A smaller note: two claws at +6 for 2d6 each average 14 damage a round. That is fine, but if both claws roll high or one crits, a level 3 wizard can go from full to 0 in one turn. On its own that is only a watch item.

## Smallest practical fix

Do two things and leave the rest of the stat block alone.

**1. Turn Drowning Song into pressure, not a kill switch** (homebrew, changed ability):

> **Drowning Song (Recharge 5 to 6).** Each creature within 30 feet that can hear the Widow makes a DC 14 Constitution saving throw. On a failure, it takes 7 (2d6) cold damage, coughs up marsh water, and is Charmed until the end of its next turn. While Charmed this way, it must spend its movement getting closer to the Widow by the safest route it can, and it can take no reactions. On a success, it takes half the damage and nothing else happens.

Characters still feel like they are drowning on dry boards and walking toward the water, and the crew gets a reason to spread out and stop up their ears. No one drops from a single save, though. Stopping up the ears with wax, cloth or mud should count as not hearing it. That gives the crew something clever to do.

**2. Add two drowned dead to fix the XP and the action economy.** Two Zombies (CR 1/4, 50 XP each, as written in the SRD) climb out of the shallows on round 1. That puts the total at 1,200 XP, inside the band (above 1,125, at or below 2,000). The zombies split the crew's attacks and soak a turn or two, so the Widow lasts long enough to sing. They already appear on the Drowned Chapel's travel line, so they fit the place.

If you want the fight closer to the High end, add up to four zombies (1,300 XP). Don't go past that unless you also lower the Widow's damage.

## How it plays at each end

- **Level 3:** with the fix, a hard fight. One or two characters may go down from claws plus a Song round, but a single save no longer drops anyone. As written, it is a coin flip on a wipe.
- **Level 5:** five level 5 characters have a Moderate budget of 3,750 XP, so even with zombies the fight is below Low. It will be quick. A wizard with *fireball* clears the zombies in one turn. The fixed Song is a nuisance at this level. The original one still drops level 5 characters, because a save-or-drop does not get easier with level, so as written the fight is trivial plus a lottery ticket.

## Other checks for this fight

- **Label it homebrew** in the dispatch: Bog Widow, homebrew CR 4, with the changed Drowning Song. Its 90 HP stands, since the profile uses monster hit points as written and this is your own stat block.
- **If it gets loud:** more drowned dead rise from the shallows each round the fight goes on near open water (one zombie at the end of each round is enough).
- **Pulling back:** the Widow does not leave the water. A crew that poles the punt out of song range is out of the fight. Keep that as a way to end it without killing, next to whatever non-combat ending the job already has.
