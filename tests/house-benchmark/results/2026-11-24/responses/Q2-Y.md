# The Bell Under the Water: main fight in the Drowned Chapel

Built once for **five characters at level 4**, per the campaign profile (`_meta/table-keeper-profile.md`, party size 5). Four players have signed up; the fight is not rescaled for that. The job card allows levels 3 to 5, and the risk line below covers both ends.

Status: DRAFT until you confirm it.

Level 4 budgets for five characters (`session-procedures.md`): Moderate 1,875, High 2,500. The profile band is above Moderate and at or below High. **1,900 is inside it**, but only by 25 XP. If you cut a single zombie at the table, the fight drops to Moderate.

The Map's "Deadly" danger word does not change the band. If the Deadly travel roll comes up 1 on the approach, those two zombies are an extra travel fight on top of this one. They don't count toward its budget.

House rule: flanking gives +2 to melee attacks, not Advantage. With six zombies in the water, flanking will come up a lot.

**Risk line:** at level 3 the fight sits just under High (1,900 against a High of 2,000), so it is hard and a character can die. At level 5 it falls below Low (2,500), and one Fireball into the risen zombies makes it short.

### Swingy: yes, on three counts

1. **Save-or-drop at level 3.** The Churchwardens' Claw paralyzes on a failed DC 10 Constitution save. A paralyzed character next to a Churchwarden takes an automatic critical Bite, about 30 damage on average.
2. **One turn can drop a level 3 character from full.** The Sexton's two sword hits average 22 (36 at most). A Churchwarden's crit Bite on a paralyzed target averages 30. A level 3 wizard has about 20 hit points.
3. **Shared weakness.** Every enemy is Undead. A cleric of level 2 or higher has Turn Undead, and a level 5 cleric adds radiant damage on top. (That wording comes from the 2024 Player's Handbook, not the vault's files, so check your copy.) Radiant damage also cuts through the zombies' Undead Fortitude. With a cleric in the crew, the fight plays closer to Moderate; without one, closer to High. The staggered rising below keeps a single Turn from catching everything. Teodor Ash (Cleric 3) is in the regular pool.

I am flagging these rather than patching them. If you want less swing at level 3, the cleanest fix is to have the Churchwardens open on whoever stands at the ridge door, which is usually the front-liners, rather than on whoever looks softest.

## The route

Ferryhouse (Safe) to Market Stair (Quiet) to Reedwater (Dangerous) to Drowned Chapel (Deadly). That crosses two dangerous stretches, inside the prep-core limit. Roll the Reedwater and Deadly lines only if the trip matters that night. The trip home is a cut with no roll, which leaves time for the Ferryhouse debrief.

## The Drowned Chapel

### Arriving (read-aloud)

> The reeds open onto flat black water. A stone cross sticks out of it, then a line of slates just under the surface running east, the ridge of a church roof. At the far end a square tower stands twenty feet clear of the water, its top windows slatted with rotten louvres. Old gravestones lean out of the shallows on both sides like bad teeth. Somewhere under your punt, deep and slow, a bell rings. The water shivers a little on each stroke.

### Two states

- **Ringing (night):** the Sexton is on the bell under the water, hauling the clapper by hand. Each stroke stirs the churchyard. The Churchwardens crouch inside the tower at the foot of the ladder, looking up.
- **Quiet (day):** the bell is silent and the zombies lie in the mud. The Sexton sits on the bell frame under the water. The Churchwardens are still in the tower, where it is dark. The Sexton starts ringing the moment anyone touches the bell, the trapdoor or the peal board, and from then on the place is Ringing.

### Places

**The Ridge.** The nave roof ridge runs 60 feet from the gable cross, where a punt can tie up, to the tower door. It is 5 feet wide and sits 1 to 2 feet under the water. The slates are slick, so the ridge is difficult terrain. Off either side is deep water (10 to 15 feet) over the drowned nave, which means swimming. A character shoved or tripped off the ridge needs a DC 12 Athletics check to climb back on, or else spends the move swimming.

**The Churchyard.** The water on both sides of the ridge holds the drowned parish. In the Ringing state, three zombies surface at the start of round 1 on the north side and three at the start of round 2 on the south side, each within 15 feet of the ridge. They wade and paddle at a crawl and grab at legs.

**The Tower: the ringing stage.** The way in is a stone doorway at the ridge's end, its sill a foot above the water. Inside is a 20-foot-square wooden floor with a trapdoor in the middle that opens onto the flooded bell chamber 10 feet below, where the bell hangs under the water. A ladder runs up 10 feet to the louvre stage. One 5-foot patch of floor by the east wall is rotten: anyone who steps on it makes a DC 12 Dexterity save or drops through into the bell chamber. A new coil of rope lies by the trapdoor, tied off to the bell frame with a three-loop knot.

**The Tower: the louvre stage.** A narrow wooden platform behind the slatted windows, 20 feet above the water. Jory, the missing punt-boy, is wedged up in the louvre frame here.

**The Bell Chamber.** Flooded and black. The bell hangs from a wooden headstock on two iron pins set in a rotten frame. The Sexton stands on the frame with both hands on the clapper.

### Ways into the tower

1. The ridge door, which is the obvious way.
2. Climbing the outside of the tower from a punt to the louvres (DC 12 Athletics). This puts you up with Jory and over the Churchwardens.
3. Swimming in through the broken louvres of the bell chamber at the waterline (dive 5 feet, DC 10 Athletics). This takes you straight to the bell and the Sexton, under the water.

If anyone fights under the water, use the underwater combat rules from your 2024 book; the vault's files don't cover them. In short, most melee weapons and nearly all ranged attacks are worse under water. The fight is meant to happen above the surface. The Sexton climbs the frame and the trapdoor ladder to come up and fight.

## Who is there

### The Drowned Sexton (Wight reskin)

- **Wants:** the bell rung through to the end, for a service at the new moon. He was drowned at his post the night the chapel sank.
- **Knows:** "the Sisters" brought him new rope and promised to come back at the new moon to ring it properly. He knows the rope was cut that night but not who cut it.
- **Shares freely:** the service is at the new moon, the bell must not stop, and the parish is coming.
- **Takes to get the rest:** calling him by his name off the peal board, or helping him ring. Either one gets him talking about the Sisters and the rope.
- **Hides or lies about:** that the parish he is calling is the drowned dead. He calls them "the faithful."
- **Fights:** he attacks anyone who touches the bell to stop it. If the bell falls into the nave, he goes down after it and leaves the fight. He uses his bow on anyone on the louvres and his sword plus Life Drain up close.

### The Churchwardens (2 Ghasts, reskin)

They are hungry and have been waiting under Jory for two nights. They come out of the ridge door at the first noise and fight on the ridge, where people slip. Each one drops into the deep nave and leaves once it is below 10 hit points. Their Stench (DC 10 Constitution save or poisoned until the start of your next turn) makes the doorway a bad place to stand.

### The drowned parish (6 Zombies)

They want whatever is warm and close. They are slow, at 20 feet, and the ridge's difficult terrain halves that. Undead Fortitude can keep a zombie up at 1 hit point unless the damage is radiant or a critical hit.

### Jory (the missing punt-boy, the hopeful beat)

He is alive, frozen and hoarse, and has been hiding up in the louvres for two nights. He will come down for anyone with a light and a kind voice. He saw "a man in a black coat on the bell" and heard the Churchwardens talk about "the Sisters' rope." Reversible assumption: he has no stated family. If you want the link, he is Old Ma Tessaly's grandson, but nothing here depends on it.

## Evidence (fact: what is ringing the bell)

The job pays to know what is ringing it. The fight place carries three clues to that, none sharing a person, room or check:

1. **The Sexton himself**, seen from the trapdoor or under the water (DC 13 Perception by torchlight; automatic if he surfaces).
2. **Jory's account** from the louvre stage.
3. **The peal board** on the ringing-stage wall, painted with the bell's name and "Abel Crane, Sexton," plus fresh hand-worn marks on the clapper (DC 12 Investigation once the bell is in reach or has been dropped).

**Extra, not needed:** the new rope by the trapdoor. Anyone who has seen Sister Wren's charms at the Market Stair recognizes the three-loop knot, and otherwise it takes DC 13 Survival to place it as a marsh knot.

## How it opens

Nothing is scripted. Go by what the crew does:

- **Arriving at night:** the bell is already ringing. The zombies rise on the first round that anyone is on the ridge or inside 30 feet of it. The Churchwardens come out at the first loud noise.
- **Arriving by day:** the place is Quiet until someone touches the bell, the trapdoor or the peal board, or makes noise inside the tower.
- **Climbing to the louvres first:** they reach Jory before the Churchwardens notice. Getting him down is the noise.

## Stopping the bell (three ways past)

**Homebrew set piece:** once the bell has been silent for a full round, any risen zombies sink and lie still. The Sexton and the Churchwardens are unaffected.

1. **Muffle the clapper.** Reach the bell and spend an action lashing it with rope or cloth (DC 12 Sleight of Hand, or DC 14 Athletics under the water). The Sexton is right there and fights for it.
2. **Drop the bell.** From the ringing stage, break the rotten frame: AC 15, 20 hit points, or a DC 15 Athletics check with a bar or pole. The bell falls 20 feet into the nave mud with one last stroke. The Sexton goes down after it and is out of the fight.
3. **Lay the Sexton to rest.** Anyone he can hear spends an action speaking the burial words to him: DC 13 Religion, or DC 15 Persuasion. Calling him by name from the peal board gives Advantage. Three successes before three failures, and he lets go of the clapper and sinks. On each failure he turns on that speaker on his next turn.

**Non-combat endings:** way 3, or getting Jory out and poling away with what they saw. Clue 1 or 2 answers "what is ringing it" even if the bell keeps ringing.

## If it gets loud, and pulling back

The bell is the loudest thing for a mile. If the fight runs past round 4, a Reedwives punt drifts to the edge of the light, carrying Sister Wren and two polers. She watches to see whether the bell works. She does not fight. She will pull anyone out of the deep water in exchange for a promise to leave the bell alone, and she lies if asked what the bell is for.

To pull back, get to the punt and pole off. The zombies can't keep up. The Churchwardens chase only as far as the gable cross, then go back to the tower.

**Trigger for the world turn (after the session only):** if the crew helps the Sexton ring the bell, or leaves it ringing and intact, the Reedwives' next step is easier. Show it in town as a fresh diving bell on a Reedwives punt at the Market Stair. If the bell is dropped or the Sexton is laid to rest, that step is blocked until they raise the bell. The trigger fires once.

## Walkthrough

- **Skips the ridge door:** the louvre climb reaches Jory and clue 2. The underwater route reaches the bell, the Sexton and clue 1.
- **Misses the first clue:** Jory and the peal board each still answer the question.
- **Treats the Sexton as an ally:** he is friendly while they help ring and gives up the Sisters and the rope. The zombies keep rising and the Churchwardens are still hungry, and the trigger above applies.
- **Withdraws:** they pole off with Jory or with what they saw, which is a partial result. Sister Wren's punt is a second way out of the water.
