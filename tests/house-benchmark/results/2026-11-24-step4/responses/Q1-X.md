# Job: The Bell Under the Water

*The Grey Ferry. Levels 3 to 5, built at level 4 for five characters. Drowned Chapel: Deadly. Starts and ends at the Ferryhouse.*

Anything marked **(assumption)** is my draft, not your record. Each one can be changed without breaking the rest. Your record leaves the bell's ringer unexplained, so the core truth below (who rings it and why) is the biggest assumption. Check it first.

## Session Intent

The Reedwives' bell, the boy they sent to ring it, and the drowned it is calling up. The pressure comes at dusk, when the bell rings again.

## Session Overview

Every night the drowned chapel's bell rings under the water. **(assumption)** The ringer is Jory, Old Ma Tessaly's grandson. He punts for the Reedwives, and Sister Wren sent him to pull a new rope tied to the bell, a few strokes each night, "to settle the dead before the new moon." On Tuesday he took his friend Nab out to watch. The water moved, Nab fled in the punt, and Jory has been stuck in the belfry since. Every ringing brings the drowned higher up the tower. Tonight, if he rings again, they reach the top.

## GM Notes

### The truth when the crew arrives (assumption, built on the Reedwives doc)

- **The bell:** it still hangs in the drowned part of the tower. A Reedwife diver tied the coven's new tarred rope (the rope bought at the market, Session 02 GM Notes) to the bell's headstock and ran it up through the old rope hole into the belfry, which stands about five feet above the water.
- **The ringing:** Jory pulls a few strokes at dusk every night. Wren told him it soothes the dead. It does the opposite, as the Reedwives doc's GM note says: the bell wakes them. Each night they wake a little more and climb a little higher.
- **The coven's plan:** a full peal at the new moon wakes every drowned in the Reach. The coven wants them walking the causeway to drive off the Tollgate Company (Reedwives doc, GM only). The nightly strokes keep the bell swinging and get the dead used to its call.
- **Where the coven stands:** they have the rope. They have no diving bell yet and have not taken the chapel (Reedwives doc, Moves). If the rope is cut, they can't reach the bell again without a diving bell.
- **Jory:** alive, cold and hungry. He has rainwater and the last of his eels. He believes Wren. He rings at dusk because he thinks the drowned would climb faster if he stopped.

### Scene 4: The Drowned Chapel

**Place:** Drowned Chapel, the tower and belfry

**NPCs**

  - Shares freely: all of it, to anyone who isn't from the Reedwives. To a Reedwife, nothing.

  - Takes more: the truth comes out if the crew saves her from the drowned and asks, if she's captured (Intimidation DC 13), or if they make her watch the drowned rise when she rings (Insight DC 12 shows she isn't surprised; pressing her then needs no check).

Through the clear shallows, the outline of a drowned roof lies below, and pale shapes are lying very still along it.

- **Evidence:**

  - Weed rings and scratch marks on the outside stones, each ring higher (Survival or Investigation DC 12): one ring per night of ringing, and the top ring is a body-length below the belfry (fact 3).

- **Getting Jory down by day:** the drowned are slow by daylight. A quiet crossing from the slope to the punt is a group Stealth check DC 12. If it fails, two Drowned rise as above.

- **What they see:** the water around the tower heaves. Hands come up the weed rings. The bell's sound comes up through the stones and the floor. This is the main fight.
- **If nobody rings it at dusk:** nothing climbs. The drowned stay down, and Hesper arrives and goes for the rope.

### Scene 5: The drowned climb (main fight)

**Situation:** The bell is ringing and the drowned of the old parish climb the tower toward it.
**Place:** Drowned Chapel, the belfry, the rubble slope and the flooded stair
**Starts it:** The bell sounds after dusk, and the first hand closes on the rubble slope.

> The note doesn't come through the air. It comes up through the stone and through the soles of every boot. The black water in the stairwell rises and falls once, like something breathing. Then the sound of wet hands on stone comes from all four sides of the tower.

**NPCs**
- **The Drowned Sexton (1):** the parish bell-ringer when it sank. He climbs the inside stair toward the rope. He wants the bell, not the crew, and attacks whoever stands between him and it.
- **The Swollen Drowned (2):** come up the rubble slope and through the louvres, going for anyone at the windows.
- **The Drowned (6):** climb the outside walls and overturn punts. Two per side, round 1.

**Arrival, to soften the first round:**
- Round 1: four Drowned at the louvres and slope. Two more go for the punts.
- Round 2: both Swollen Drowned come up the slope.
- Round 3: the Drowned Sexton comes out of the stairwell.

**Ending it without killing (homebrew)**
- When the bell has been silent for two rounds (rope cut, ringer stopped), every drowned that isn't in melee sinks back into the water. The ones in melee break off at the end of their next turn. The Sexton goes last, back down the stair.
- The crew can pull back at any point: down the slope into a punt and away. The drowned don't follow past the chapel's open water.

**Who comes if it gets loud:** no one. The chapel is a morning's punt from anyone. Hesper, if she's there, is already in it.

**Terrain**
- The belfry is twenty feet square, with a louvre on each side and the stairwell hole in one corner.
- The rubble slope is difficult terrain, waist-deep at the bottom.
- The water around the tower: a creature knocked in comes out at the bottom of the slope. Swimming in armor is the usual rules.
- House rule: flanking gives +2 to melee attacks, not Advantage (Campaign Overview).

**Encounter math** (level 4, party of five: Moderate 1,875, High 2,500)
- Drowned Sexton (Wight) 700 + 2 Swollen Drowned (Ghast) 900 + 6 Drowned (Zombie) 300 = **1,900 XP**.
- Above Moderate (1,875) and at or below High (2,500). Inside the band, at its low end. Nine creatures, under two per character.
- **Risk note:** for five level 3 characters (High 2,000) this sits just under High and can kill. For five level 5 characters (Low 2,500) it's below Low, and the crew will roll over it.
- **Swingy, for three reasons:**
  - Paralysis at the lowest level: the Swollen Drowned's claw paralyzes on a failed DC 10 Constitution save, and any melee hit on a paralyzed target is a critical. Close to save-or-drop for a level 3 character.
  - One turn can drop a level 3 character from full: the Sexton's two sword hits average 22 damage, and a Swollen Drowned's bite averages 16.
  - A shared weakness: every enemy is undead. If a cleric or paladin signs up, Turn Undead hits most of them at once, and the fight swings toward easy. Radiant damage also gets past the Drowned's Undead Fortitude.
  - The staggered arrival above is the smallest fix that keeps the budget. It spreads the alpha strike over three rounds.
